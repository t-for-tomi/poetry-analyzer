For a moment,
I thought I needed a dagger.

Something sharp enough
to return the wound,
something that would make the other hand
understand the anatomy of its own cruelty.

I searched for a weapon,
a sentence with teeth,
a silence that could bruise,
a departure loud enough to be remembered.

But somewhere in the pursuit of retaliation,
I caught myself.

When did I become so unfamiliar with tenderness
that my first instinct was to sharpen?

I used to know how to speak.
Not necessarily in paragraphs,
not in those carefully assembled explanations
where every sentence begs to be understood.

I mean expression.
The kind that comes unannounced,
unrehearsed,
without having to translate the ache
into something another person might finally respect.

Perhaps I have spent too long explaining my wounds
to people who were comfortable with the bleeding.

And now, even my silence feels borrowed.

I don't know when I forgot
that not every hurt deserves an answer,
that not every wound requires a weapon,
that I can leave a room
without setting it on fire.

For a moment, I thought I needed a dagger.

Perhaps what I needed
was to find my hands again
and remember what they were made for
