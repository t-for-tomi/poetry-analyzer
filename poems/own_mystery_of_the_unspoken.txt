I said give me a weapon,

and you thought a sword hmm,

something that cuts clean,

something that looks like war.

I said lend me a weapon,

and you sought a spear hmm,

something pointed outward,

something that keeps distance between me and pain.

I said make me a weapon,

and you said my voice,

and placed a pen in my palm

like you were teaching thunder how to write itself.

But tell me,

on the table of whose heart 

shall it be made known

the aches of a young child

who learned too early that silence can bleed?

On whose tablet shall it be written,

the trembling grammar of growing youth,

who speaks in half-courage

and swallowed sentences?

Under what moon

shall the tales of glory emerge?

not the polished kind they clap for,

but the kind that limps into daylight

with dirt still under its fingernails?

And if my voice is the weapon,

why does it tremble

before it learns its own name?

Why does truth feel like guilt

before it feels like freedom?

Still….

I write.

Not because I am ready,

but because silence

has already finished its war on me

and lost nothing.

So let the ink fall like verdicts.

Let it bruise the page gently.

Let it say what my mouth still negotiates with fear.

Because maybe the real weapon was never destruction,

maybe it was the courage

to name what should not have survived inside me.
