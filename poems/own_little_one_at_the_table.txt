Knock, knock!

All ye doors of wisdom and gates of understanding,
lend me your hinges, if only for a moment.

For there is a little one sitting at the table of misconception,
feasting on half truths,
drinking from cups filled by borrowed convictions,
and calling it knowledge.

She has mistaken familiarity for understanding,
assumption for revelation,
and the echoes of other people's voices
for the sound of truth itself.

Oh, but how does one know what one does not know?
How does a mind recognise its own blindness
when it has grown accustomed to the dark?

So, I come knocking.

Not with the arrogance of one who has found all the answers,
but with the trembling curiosity of one
who has finally realised
that perhaps the questions were never wrong.

Open, then,
the doors that ignorance has kept shut.
Unteach me where I have learnt wrongly.
Challenge the things I have held sacred
simply because I have held them for so long.

For I would rather be humbled by the truth
than be comforted by a beautiful lie.

And if wisdom has a seat at this table,
pray, let there be room for one more.

I am only a little one,
but I have come hungry.
